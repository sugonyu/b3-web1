## Goal

My goal was to present BookLoop's client need, privacy-focused scope, design

use the feedback to choose a safer Deliverable 2 scope.

## What I Faced and Attempted

I presented BookLoop as a Korean-language book-sharing platform for Montreal.
The main design problem is not only tracking whether a book is available. The
application must help people build trust without publishing private contact or
meeting information too early.

Muhamed suggested deposit or card-based protection against unreturned books, a
community donation area, Korean flag-inspired colors, and social links in the
footer. Kamyar emphasized that a privacy-focused service needs reporting and
administrative review in addition to ratings. He recommended focusing the scope
on approval, privacy, trust, and an Admin View, and strongly advised against
implementing payment integration within the course timeline.

## What I Achieved

- Presented the required Deliverable 1 definition and design package.
- Confirmed that privacy and trust are the project's differentiating concerns.
- Recorded reporting and administrative review as important future workflow
  requirements.
- Excluded payment, card storage, deposits, refunds, and charging from the Web1
  implementation scope.
- Identified the Deliverable 2 focus: a working Flask application, relational
  database, validation, authentication where required, and one database-backed
  create/read vertical slice.

## Decisions and Remaining Risk

Ratings alone are not a complete safety system. A future reporting workflow must
include a review state and an authorized administrator action, not only a report
button. Private contact information must remain protected until an approved
relationship exists.

The main Week 2 risk is expanding into moderation features before the backend
foundation is demonstrable. Deliverable 2 will establish the database-backed
borrow-request boundary first. Reporting and Admin View remain documented scope,
not claims of completed functionality.
