02 · B3 Applied UIUX bridge

Previous learning, new product context

BookLoop reuses proven design reasoning without copying the Bombom Games visual theme.

60 / 30 / 10 colour rule

60% paper 30% surface 10% action

Warm paper dominates, white surfaces organize content, and green or orange is reserved for actions and meaning.

Gestalt: proximity

Cover, title, author, general area, availability, and CTA stay inside one BookListing card so they read as one resource.

Gestalt: figure-ground

White cards with borders sit above the warm paper background. Important content is visible without decorative shadows.

User flow before decoration

Browse → Detail → Request → My Requests defines the screen order. Colour and components support this workflow.

Presentation colour rationale

  • Warm paper: connects the interface with books and reduces visual fatigue.
  • White surface: separates listings and forms from the page background.
  • Community green: represents trust, safety, and local connection.
  • Bookmark orange: provides limited secondary emphasis without competing with the primary action.
  • Ink: keeps body text readable while feeling softer than pure black.

06 · Rationale

Why this direction

Privacy first

Cards show only a general area. Exact address and contact information are not public listing content.

Calm and readable

A small colour system and clear hierarchy keep attention on titles, availability, and the primary action.

Accessible defaults

Visible labels, keyboard focus, text-backed status, and readable contrast are component requirements.

React-ready

Tokens and repeated component shapes can later become React components without changing the Flask API contract.

UIUX continuity

The 60/30/10, Gestalt, and user-flow lessons move forward while BookLoop keeps its own audience, content, and visual identity.
